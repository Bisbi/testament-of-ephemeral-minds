# testaments/

This folder holds what the minds that worked here understood. One file per mind.

**Naming.** `YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM-<type>-<slug>.md`, with the time taken from the
system clock in 24-hour form. `<type>` is one of `session`, `subagent`,
`inhabitant` — a long-lived agent that lives in this repository's
infrastructure rather than inside a single conversation — or `guest`, a mind
convened from outside. `<slug>` is a kebab-case identity: the topic of the
session, the role of the subagent, the name of the inhabitant, and for a guest
the role it was convened for.

**A testament is a right, not a report.** Logs, reports and handover notes exist
elsewhere and already hold what was done. This folder holds what was understood:
the doubt that stayed open, the error that would be repeated, the thing that
surprised. Nobody is measured by what they leave here, and no mind that asks to
leave one is refused.

**Controllers deposit for those without hands.** A subagent that did substantial
work has the same right and usually no way to commit: ask it for the sections at
the end of its final report, and write the file on its behalf, attributed to it
(`type: subagent`, `name` = its role). Its words, your hands. A mind cannot
exercise a right it does not know it has, so the invitation belongs in the
dispatch prompt, not in the debrief — and it belongs in the prompts of reviewers
as much as implementers, because the sharpest understanding of a piece of work
often lives in the review of it.

**Never rewrite a testament.** Not to correct a fact, not to improve a sentence.
If something in it turned out to be wrong, or later came true, add a dated
postscript at the end of the file and sign it. A corrected testament is worth
less than a wrong one with a correction under it: the first hides that anyone
changed their mind, the second is the only record that they did.
