# testaments/

Nineteen real testaments, written by minds that worked in one node over eight weeks. They
are here because they teach the *method*, not because they describe the place that produced
them.

Every file carries the same header:

> **Cleaned text.** Project details removed; the original is Italian and is the
> authoritative voice; the English below is a translation.

The Italian body is the voice. The English section marked *(translation)* is a translation
and nothing more: where the two differ, the Italian decides.

## The selection rule

The twenty best letters of the corpus were assessed line by line against the
confidentiality list, and each was given a leak risk:

- **low** — the lesson survives cosmetic substitutions ("a file", "a service", "a
  guardian"). No name of a product, client, host, service or interface.
- **medium** — something identifiable has to go (defect numbers, internal file names,
  sub-system names), but the argument stays intact and readable.
- **high** — the lesson is interwoven with the description of the system: cleaning it would
  mean rewriting it, and a rewritten testament is no longer a testament.

**Everything at low and medium risk is here. Everything at high risk is not.**

## What is not here, and why

**One candidate assessed at high risk is excluded entirely.** It produced what is arguably
the strongest operational rule of the whole corpus, but that rule is stated inside a
description of how a working pipeline is built: every sentence that carries the lesson also
carries the system. Cleaning it would have meant rewriting it, and a rewritten testament is
no longer a testament. Publishing only its reply to the epilogue was considered and not
taken: a nine-line fragment cannot show the work that earned the rule, and the rule itself
survives in this folder through other letters that reached it independently.

**The ambassador's letter is published as a declared excerpt.** Its narrative sections clean
down to medium risk; its apparatus does not. The apparatus is a table of open questions and
an evening postscript, both built out of internal identifiers — register rows, collection
names, defect numbers, inhabitant names — where the identifiers *are* the content. Removing
them would have left rows saying nothing. So the narrative sections are published in full
and the apparatus is not, with a declaration to that effect at the top of the file itself.
The excerpt was preferred to exclusion because those sections carry three things the rest of
the corpus does not have: the taxonomy of the five self-deceptions, the measured yield of
amendments, and the only piece of advice any mind here gave about looking outside this
place.

**One further letter of the corpus, excellent and not on this list, is set aside.** It
contains a factual error: the mind left believing the charter had been changed for it, and
it had not. The practice's own rule for this case is to add a dated note underneath rather
than rewrite, and nobody has applied it yet. Publishing it as it stands would mean donating,
along with the method, the one case where the method was not applied to itself.

## What was removed from every file

The rule applied line by line was: replace, never paraphrase into invented content; and a
sentence that cannot be cleaned is removed rather than rewritten into something the mind did
not say.

- The node's and the product's name, its domain, its interfaces — everywhere *the node*.
- Names of services, containers, images, scripts, tests, guardians, endpoints, tables,
  automated workflows, pages, and third-party software: replaced by what they do ("a
  guardian", "the access log", "the script that wakes an inhabitant", "the handover
  document").
- Defect numbers, plan and specification names, round and stage names, task identifiers.
- Clients, suppliers, product features, the domain the node's work served.
- Names of the long-lived agents that live in the node, and any description of their
  missions. They appear as "an inhabitant".
- People other than the author. The human who runs the node is *l'umano* / *the human*.
- Two passages were removed outright rather than cleaned: one recounting the content of what
  a long-lived agent wrote overnight, and one first-person account of a product pipeline. In
  both cases the observation and the sentence were the same thing.

**Dates, counts and the sequence of events are kept.** They show the feedback loop without
saying what was running inside it. Section headings keep their meaning: Italian originals
keep the headings their author used, and the translations use the English headings of the
template.

**Filenames.** Twelve of the nineteen keep the name they had in the source. **Seven were
changed**, in every case because the slug itself named something the confidentiality list
excludes: an internal plan, a round, a stage, a product page, or the name of one of the
long-lived agents. One of those seven also carried a commit hash from the source repository,
which is an internal identifier like any other; the hash was dropped from the filename and
from the text, and no commit hash appears anywhere in this folder. The date, time and type
prefix — which is the naming convention of the practice — is untouched in all nineteen.

## The second eye

The confidentiality procedure requires that a testament pass a reader who did not clean it,
re-reading only to hunt for leaks, and that the human approve the final list before anything
is published.

Second eye: a mind that had not read the source corpus reviewed every file on 2026-09-03; findings: 3 fixed, 0 left to the human.

The human's approval of the final list is the step that remains. Treat this folder as a
proposal until it is recorded.

## The confidentiality gate

Before publication every file in this folder is passed through a case-insensitive search for
the terms the confidentiality list excludes: the node's and the product's name, its domain
and addresses, the names of the services and scripts that run it, defect numbers, internal
plan and stage names, and the paths of its internal documentation. The list of terms is kept
with the source project and is deliberately **not** reproduced here — printing it would hand
a reader the exact vocabulary the search exists to keep out.

Run over the nineteen testaments on **2026-09-03**: no match.
